# 🎓 Courses and Video Lectures

## Why This Section Matters

Great lectures can make difficult concepts click in ways that textbooks alone cannot. These courses range from full university lecture courses (MIT OCW, Yale, Stanford's Susskind, Cornell's Strogatz) to beautifully produced visual explanations (3Blue1Brown) and hands-on quantum computing tutorials (Qiskit). Use them alongside the textbooks — they are not a replacement, but a powerful complement.

## Suggested Order

1. **3Blue1Brown — Essence of Linear Algebra + Essence of Calculus** — Mathematical foundations. Watch these first, regardless of your background. They build visual intuition that will pay dividends throughout.
2. **Feynman Lectures Online** — Use as a companion throughout Sections 00–05 (plus Vol. 3 Ch. 13–14 for Section 08 and Vol. 2 Ch. 38–41 for Section 09). Read each chapter as you reach the corresponding topic.
3. **MIT 8.01SC + Shankar's Yale PHYS 201** — Full lecture courses for mechanics and E&M. Watch alongside Feynman for problem-solving technique and physical intuition. (Walter Lewin's MIT 8.01/8.02 lectures, once the standard pick here, were removed by MIT in December 2014 after it found he had sexually harassed an online learner; MIT no longer hosts them.)
4. **Susskind — Theoretical Minimum series** — A second perspective on classical and quantum mechanics for deeper understanding.
5. **3Blue1Brown — Fourier Transform** — Watch during Section 01 (Waves and Optics), then MIT 8.03SC once you have done first-year E&M.
6. **MIT 8.04 (Zwiebach)** — Pair with Griffiths QM in Section 04.
7. **MIT 8.333 (Kardar)** — Advanced statistical mechanics. Watch after completing Section 02 basics.
8. **Simon — The Oxford Solid State Basics** — Pair with Simon's textbook in Section 08.
9. **Strogatz — Nonlinear Dynamics lectures** — Watch alongside the Strogatz textbook in Section 10.
10. **Watrous — Understanding Quantum Information & Computation** — IBM's successor to the Qiskit Textbook, with Qiskit code examples. Pair with Section 07.
11. **MIT 18.085 (Strang)** — Computational methods for Sections 06 and 09.
