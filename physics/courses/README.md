# 🎓 Courses and Video Lectures

## Why This Section Matters

Great lectures can make difficult concepts click in ways that textbooks alone cannot. These courses range from legendary classroom recordings (Lewin, Susskind, Strogatz) to beautifully produced visual explanations (3Blue1Brown) and hands-on quantum computing tutorials (Qiskit). Use them alongside the textbooks — they are not a replacement, but a powerful complement.

## Suggested Order

1. **3Blue1Brown — Essence of Linear Algebra + Essence of Calculus** — Mathematical foundations. Watch these first, regardless of your background. They build visual intuition that will pay dividends throughout.
2. **Feynman Lectures Online** — Use as a companion throughout Sections 00–05. Read each chapter as you reach the corresponding topic.
3. **Walter Lewin — MIT 8.01 + 8.02** — The legendary lecture demonstrations. Watch alongside Feynman for problem-solving technique and physical intuition.
4. **Susskind — Theoretical Minimum series** — A second perspective on classical and quantum mechanics for deeper understanding.
5. **3Blue1Brown — Fourier Transform** — Watch during Section 01 (Waves and Optics).
6. **MIT 8.04 (Zwiebach)** — Pair with Griffiths QM in Section 04.
7. **MIT 8.333 (Kardar)** — Advanced statistical mechanics. Watch after completing Section 02 basics.
8. **Strogatz — Nonlinear Dynamics lectures** — Watch alongside the Strogatz textbook in Section 10.
9. **Qiskit Textbook** — Hands-on quantum computing with real quantum hardware. Pair with Section 07.
10. **MIT 18.085 (Strang)** — Computational methods for Sections 06 and 09.
