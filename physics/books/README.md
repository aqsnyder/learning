# 📚 Books

## Why This Section Matters

These are the textbooks that form the backbone of a serious physics education. Each has been selected for clarity, depth, and lasting value. The must-read titles (🔑) are the ones you should prioritize — they are widely regarded as the best in their domains and will be referenced throughout the curriculum.

## Suggested Reading Order

1. **Feynman Lectures, Vol. 1** — Start here. Read the mechanics, thermodynamics, and waves chapters as you work through Sections 00–02. Feynman builds unmatched physical intuition.
2. **Taylor — Classical Mechanics** — Your primary mechanics textbook. Work through it alongside the Feynman Lectures.
3. **Schroeder — An Introduction to Thermal Physics** — The clearest introduction to thermodynamics and statistical mechanics.
4. **Griffiths — Introduction to Electrodynamics** — The gold standard for undergraduate E&M. Griffiths' writing is a model of clarity.
5. **Feynman Lectures, Vol. 2** — Read alongside Griffiths for complementary perspectives on electromagnetism.
6. **Griffiths & Schroeter — Introduction to Quantum Mechanics** — The natural next step. Same beautiful writing applied to quantum mechanics.
7. **Feynman Lectures, Vol. 3** — Feynman's unique take on quantum mechanics. Complements Griffiths beautifully.
8. **Newman — Computational Physics** — Learn to simulate the physics you've studied. Best paired with Section 06.
9. **Kittel — Introduction to Solid State Physics** — The standard reference for condensed matter. Read alongside Section 08.
10. **Strogatz — Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos** — One of the best-written physics textbooks ever. A joy to read.
11. **Nielsen & Chuang — Quantum Computation and Quantum Information** — The bible of quantum computing. Dense but essential.
12. **Brunton & Kutz — Data-Driven Science and Engineering** — Bridges classical physics and modern data-driven methods.
13. **Ellingson — Electromagnetics** and **Hu — Modern Semiconductor Devices** — The two free engineer's bridges: from Griffiths-level E&M to transmission lines, waveguides, and antennas, and from band theory to the diodes and transistors on your boards. Read them after Sections 03 and 08 respectively.
14. **Keithley — Low Level Measurements Handbook** — The bench reference for Section 12: leakage, guarding, thermoelectric EMFs, and four-wire technique, each with the physics behind it.
