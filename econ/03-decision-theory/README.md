# Decision Theory

## Why This Section Matters

Decision theory studies how individuals make choices under uncertainty — and how they systematically deviate from "rational" models. Expected utility theory, formalized by von Neumann and Morgenstern, provides the normative benchmark. Prospect theory, developed by Kahneman and Tversky, provides the descriptive reality: people are loss-averse, overweight small probabilities, and anchor to reference points. Understanding both the normative and descriptive frameworks is essential for mechanism design (which assumes rational agents), behavioral economics (which relaxes that assumption), and AI alignment (where reward specification mirrors utility specification). This section bridges the formal and the psychological.

## Suggested Order

1. **Kahneman — Thinking, Fast and Slow** — Start here for the most accessible and comprehensive overview of the field.
2. **Tversky & Kahneman — "Heuristics and Biases" (1974)** — Read the original paper on cognitive biases.
3. **Kahneman & Tversky — "Prospect Theory" (1979)** — Read the foundational paper on how people actually make decisions under risk.
4. **von Neumann & Morgenstern — Theory of Games (Ch. 1–3)** — Understand the normative expected utility benchmark.
5. **Knight — Risk, Uncertainty and Profit** — Read for the important risk vs. uncertainty distinction.
6. **Ariely — Behavioral Economics (Coursera)** — Watch for engaging examples of systematic irrationality.
7. **Hastie & Dawes — Rational Choice in an Uncertain World** — Read for a more formal textbook treatment.
8. **Savage — The Foundations of Statistics** — Reference for the axiomatic foundations of subjective expected utility.
