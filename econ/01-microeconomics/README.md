# Microeconomics

## Why This Section Matters

Microeconomics is the formal study of individual decision-making and market outcomes. Consumer theory explains how people allocate scarce budgets; producer theory explains how firms minimize costs and maximize profits; and equilibrium analysis shows how prices emerge from the interaction of supply and demand. Welfare economics then asks whether these outcomes are efficient and how policy interventions affect social welfare. This is the mathematical backbone that supports everything from game theory to mechanism design — you cannot do serious economics without it.

## Suggested Order

1. **Khan Academy Microeconomics** — Review the modules on consumer/producer theory if you need visual reinforcement.
2. **Varian — Intermediate Microeconomics** — Work through the textbook systematically. This is the core of the section.
3. **MIT 14.01 (OCW)** — Watch Gruber's lectures alongside or after Varian for a different exposition.
4. **Hayek — "The Use of Knowledge in Society"** — Read this essay to understand the informational role of prices.
5. **Coase — "The Problem of Social Cost"** — Read for the foundational perspective on externalities and property rights.
6. **Friedman — Price Theory** — Skim for an alternative, more intuitive perspective on micro.
7. **Varian — Calculus companion** — Work through if you want the formal mathematical treatment.
8. **MWG — Microeconomic Theory** — Reference as needed for graduate-level rigor.
