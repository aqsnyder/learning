# Genetics and Genomics

## Why This Section Matters

Genetics and genomics connect the molecular machinery of the cell to inheritance, evolution, and disease. From Mendel's pea plants to the Human Genome Project to modern GWAS studies, this field tells the story of how genetic variation shapes organisms and populations. Understanding Mendelian genetics, linkage, molecular genetics, genome sequencing technologies, and epigenetics is essential for interpreting modern biomedical research, understanding precision medicine, and working with the large-scale genomic datasets that power computational biology. This section bridges classical genetics with the genomic revolution, providing the conceptual framework needed for population genetics, bioinformatics, and disease research.

## Suggested Order

1. **Mukherjee "The Gene"** — Start with the narrative history to build context and motivation for the field
2. **MIT 7.03 Genetics** — Work through Mendelian genetics, linkage, and molecular genetics systematically
3. **Watson "Molecular Biology of the Gene"** — Read selected chapters alongside the course for molecular depth
4. **NHGRI Resources** — Explore genomics fundamentals and ethical dimensions of genetic research
5. **Lander et al. Human Genome Paper** — Read the landmark paper to understand the scope and impact of the Human Genome Project
6. **Broad Institute GWAS Primer** — Understand how genome-wide studies connect genotype to phenotype
7. **1000 Genomes Project** — Explore human genetic variation data and population genetics concepts
8. **Griffiths "Introduction to Genetic Analysis"** — Use for additional problems and deeper treatment of genetic analysis methods
