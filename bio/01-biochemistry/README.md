# Biochemistry

## Why This Section Matters

Biochemistry bridges the gap between the abstract information flow of molecular biology and the physical, chemical reality of living systems. Proteins are not just gene products — they are molecular machines whose function depends on their three-dimensional structure, which in turn depends on amino acid chemistry. Understanding protein structure levels (primary through quaternary), enzyme kinetics, metabolic pathways, and biological thermodynamics is essential for drug design, protein engineering, and interpreting the outputs of structure prediction tools like AlphaFold. This section provides the chemical intuition needed to understand why proteins fold, how enzymes catalyze reactions, and how cells harness energy.

## Suggested Order

1. **Lehninger "Principles of Biochemistry"** (Chapters 1–6) — Start with amino acid chemistry, water, and thermodynamics foundations
2. **MIT 5.07SC Biological Chemistry I** — Work through lectures and problem sets alongside textbook reading
3. **PDB-101 Resources** — Explore protein structure visualizations to build 3D intuition early
4. **AK Lectures Biochemistry** — Use for targeted review of difficult topics like enzyme kinetics and Michaelis-Menten
5. **HHMI BioInteractive** — Watch enzyme and protein visualizations to reinforce mechanistic understanding
6. **Stryer "Biochemistry"** — Cross-reference for metabolic pathways and enzyme regulation
7. **Dill & MacCallum Review** — Read for an overview of the protein folding problem and its significance
8. **Voet & Voet "Biochemistry"** — Consult for the deepest treatment of thermodynamics and enzyme mechanisms
