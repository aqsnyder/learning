# PCB Layout Fundamentals

## Why This Section Matters

This is the core skill — placing components and routing traces. Everything you've learned about schematics, stackups, and materials converges here. Good layout is part engineering, part art: component placement alone determines 90% of whether a board will work well. This section builds the hands-on muscle memory and design intuition that separates a schematic that *could* work from a PCB that *does* work.

- [Section 00 — Electronics Fundamentals](../00-electronics-fundamentals/README.md)

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

After this section you can:
- Plan placement from current loops and sensitivity — high-di/dt loops, high-impedance and Kelvin nodes, connector and mechanical constraints — and place to minimize loop area and coupling before routing anything.
- Encode a fab's capabilities and your electrical constraints in KiCad (Board Setup, netclasses, custom `.kicad_dru` rules) so that DRC enforces them, and prove each rule fires.
- Lay out precision analog structures: 4-wire (Kelvin) connections to shunts and references, driven guard rings and planes around high-impedance nodes, and mask/slot features that interrupt leakage.
- Use copper pours deliberately (zone priority, clearance, thermal relief, island removal, stitching) and predict when a pour changes a trace's impedance.

## Suggested Order

**Core path (🔑):** Phil's Lab #166 (KiCad 9 PCB layout) → Feranec's layout and routing videos → KiCad 10 PCB Editor manual

1. **KiCad 10 PCB Editor manual** — Get comfortable with the tool you'll use for every exercise; read "Configuring design rules", "Custom design rules" and "Working with zones" before Practices 1 and 2
2. **Phil's Lab #166** — Watch the full placement-to-routing workflow on a small board, then the larger end-to-end STM32 board in #65
3. **Placement before routing** — Place first and route later: fix connectors and mechanical parts, keep high-di/dt loops (switcher input loop, decoupling) tight, and keep high-impedance and Kelvin nodes away from noisy edges and connectors. Most of a layout's quality is decided here (Practice 3)
4. **AN2867 crystal/oscillator layout** — Apply it to the first MCU board you place
5. **Feranec's layout and routing videos** — Professional technique, and a review of a beginner's layout and its mistakes
6. **Your fab's capabilities as rules** — Encode them in Board Setup and custom rules, using the IPC-2221 clearance-calculator article for high-voltage spacing (Practice 2)
7. **Copper zones** — Priority, clearance, thermal relief and island removal: when a pour helps and when it hurts (Practice 1)
8. **Altium Academy #9 routing tips** — The same ideas in another EDA tool

## 🛠️ Practice

1. **Pours vs controlled impedance** — With KiCad's PCB Calculator (or Saturn PCB Toolkit), compute Z₀ for a 50 Ω microstrip on your 4-layer stackup, then as grounded coplanar waveguide with pour gaps of 0.15, 0.25, 0.5, and 1.0 mm. Find the gap at which Z₀ stays within 2 % of the bare microstrip and turn it into a zone-clearance rule for your controlled-impedance netclasses. Deliverable: table + the rule committed to your project template. *(~1.5 h · KiCad Calculator Tools (TransLine; Section 01), KiCad 10 PCB Editor manual (zones, custom rules))*
2. **Fab rules as code** — Encode JLCPCB's (and one other fab's) published 4/6-layer capabilities in Board Setup plus a `.kicad_dru` file: track/space by copper weight, via drill and annular ring, hole-to-hole, copper-to-edge, mask web, silk-to-pad, and a high-voltage netclass clearance taken from IPC-2221's conductor-spacing table (Table 6-1 in Rev A; the standard is paid, so use the free Peterson clearance-calculator article, which covers Rev B and Rev C (2023, the current revision as of October 2026), and cross-check with KiCad's Electrical Spacing table) (plus a creepage rule on KiCad 9+). Safety note: IPC-2221 spacing is a design guideline, not a safety rating. For mains-connected or user-accessible hazardous voltages, creepage, clearance and insulation come from the product safety standard (IEC 61010-1 for measurement equipment, IEC 62368-1 for IT/AV), so don't treat this rule as a safety design. Build a scrap board with one deliberate violation per rule. Deliverable: the rule file in your project template + the test board. Done when every violation fires exactly one DRC error and nothing else does. *(~3 h · KiCad 10 PCB Editor manual (design rules, custom rules, DRC), Peterson's IPC-2221 clearance calculator, KiCad Calculator Tools (Section 01), JLCPCB capabilities (Section 09))*
3. **Place-only study** — For a board with an STM32U5, USB-C, a buck and two LDOs, an HSE crystal, SWD, an SPI Ethernet controller with magnetics and RJ45, and a sensor connector, make three placements without routing. Score each on switcher hot-loop area, decoupling distance to power pins (mm), crystal trace length, connectors on board edges, separation of noisy and high-impedance regions, ratsnest crossings, and test-point access. Route the winner. Deliverable: three screenshots, the scoring sheet, and a DRC-clean routed board. *(~10 h: three placements plus one full route · Phil's Lab #166 and #65, Feranec's layout videos, AN2867, AN-136 (Section 05))*
4. **Kelvin and guard block** — Lay out a precision sense block: a 4-terminal shunt (or a 2-terminal shunt with Kelvin taps from the inner pad edges) routed as a tight sense pair to a difference amplifier, plus a high-impedance input enclosed by a driven guard ring on both outer layers with solder mask opened over it, a guard pour under the node on the next layer, and optionally a routed slot. Use a net-tie footprint so the sense nets join the shunt pads without DRC shorts, and a custom rule that keeps non-guard copper away. Deliverable: layout + rationale + a leakage estimate for a 5 V neighbor across 10¹⁰ Ω and 10¹² Ω surface paths, with and without the guard. *(~3 h · KiCad 10 PCB Editor manual (custom rules, zones), The Art of Electronics Ch. 5 (Section 00), Section 12's precision-measurement resources)*

**🏗️ Mini-project — Precision Channel Layout Block:** Design one precision measurement channel as a hierarchical sheet plus layout — force amplifier with Kelvin force/sense outputs, range resistors inside guard rings, high-Z sense buffer with guard driver, ADC driver, local decoupling — then replicate it to four channels with KiCad's multichannel (9+) or PCB design-block (10) tools. Use your Exercise 2 rules plus channel-specific custom rules (guard clearances, Kelvin pair coupling, keepouts under range resistors). Deliverable: `projects/pcb/04-precision-channel/` with the KiCad files and a one-page layout rationale. Success: DRC-clean; identical routing across channels; every high-Z node enclosed by guard on both outer layers and on the inner layer beneath it; and a reviewer (or you after 48 h) can trace each force and sense path from the schematic notes without asking. *(~12 h)*
