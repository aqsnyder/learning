# PCB Anatomy and Materials

> **Why this section matters:** Understanding what a PCB physically IS before you design one.

**Prerequisites:** [Section 00 — Electronics Fundamentals](../00-electronics-fundamentals/README.md)

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

After this section you can:
- Walk through multilayer fabrication — core/prepreg lamination, drilling, desmear, electroless and electrolytic plating, imaging and etch, solder mask, surface finish, electrical test — and name the design rule each step creates.
- Compute copper thickness, sheet resistance, trace resistance and IR drop, and IPC-2221 current capacity, and explain why IPC-2152 replaced IPC-2221's conductor-sizing charts (whose internal-layer curves were never tested).
- Choose between standard FR-4, high-Tg/high-Td FR-4, low-loss, and PTFE-class laminates from datasheet Dk/Df vs frequency, Tg, Td, z-axis CTE, and moisture absorption, and specify the choice on a fab drawing by its IPC-4101 slash sheet.
- Critique a legacy design guide, separating durable physics from obsolete process advice and contested folklore.

## Suggested Order

**Core path (🔑):** Dave Jones's *PCB Design Tutorial* (2004) → Sierra Circuits' *PCB Material Selection* → the Isola 370HR and Rogers RO4350B data sheets

1. **PCB Design Tutorial Rev A** — Read it end to end (25 pages) for a mental model of PCB structure: layers, vias, plating, substrates and finishes. Treat it as a 2004 snapshot: later sections correct its grounding and output-file advice.
2. **Copper for PCBs** — Copper weight, thickness and foil types; then do Practice 1.
3. **How to Choose PCB Laminates and Materials for Fabrication** — How the laminate changes lamination, drilling and desmear. For each fabrication step in Objective 1, note the design rule or cost it creates.
4. **PCB Material Selection** — The properties that decide a laminate: Tg, Td, CTE, Dk, Df and moisture absorption.
5. **Isola 370HR and Rogers RO4350B data sheets** — Read real numbers and see how Dk and Df move with frequency, then do Practice 3 with your own fab's materials.
6. **IPC-4101 slash sheets** — How a fab note names a laminate class (you need this for the mini-project). IPC-4101 itself is paid.
7. **Altium Academy: Do You Need Low Dk PCB Materials?** — A short check on when a low-Dk material earns its cost.
8. **KiCad Calculator Tools** — Its Track Width tool uses the IPC-2221 formulas: check Practice 2's code against it.
9. **IPC-2221** — IPC sells it; the current revision is IPC-2221C (2023, as of October 2026), and the section and table numbers used here follow Rev A (2003). Each exercise below that cites it names a free route; buy it if you need its electrical clearance (Table 6-1), land, annular-ring, hole and test-coupon requirements in full. For trace width vs. current, use IPC-2152 (Section 05), not the Fig. 6-4 charts.

## 🛠️ Practice

1. **Copper arithmetic, checked 4-wire** — Compute 1 oz copper's thickness (µm and mil) and its sheet resistance at 25 °C, and use them to reproduce the milliohms-per-inch column of the track-width table in *PCB Design Tutorial Rev A*. Then pick a long, uniform trace (≥ 50 mm) on one of your boards, predict its resistance (copper tempco ≈ +0.39 %/°C), and measure it 4-wire with a precision DMM or SMU. Deliverable: calc sheet + measurement. Done when you match the tutorial within ~5 % and the measurement within ~15 %, and can explain the sign of the error (plating, etch, temperature). *(~2 h · PCB Design Tutorial Rev A, Copper for PCBs; finished copper thickness after etch and plating is in IPC-2221A §10.1, Tables 10-1 and 10-2, if you have the paid standard)*
2. **IPC-2221 vs IPC-2152** — Implement IPC-2221's I = k·ΔT^0.44·A^0.725 (§6.2 of Rev A; k = 0.048 external, 0.024 internal; A in mil²) in Python, check a few points against KiCad's Track Width calculator (which uses the IPC-2221 formulas), and plot width vs current (1–10 A, ΔT = 10 °C, 1 oz) for outer and inner layers. Overlay the tutorial's table and an IPC-2152 calculator (e.g., Saturn PCB Toolkit, with and without its plane modifier), then explain the gaps from the history of the charts: IPC-2221A Appendix B traces the external chart to a 1956 NBS report and says the internal-layer curves were never tested (the standard is paid; the IPC-2221 row above summarizes the appendix). Deliverable: plot + a 150-word recommendation for carrying 3 A on an inner layer of a 6-layer board. Done when your IPC-2221 curve reproduces the tutorial's 1 oz column within ~20 % (it is the same 1950s data; the rounded 1 A row is the worst, about 19 % off) and your memo explains how the internal-layer curves were derived. *(~2 h · IPC-2221 (formula above; Appendix B only in the paid standard), KiCad Calculator Tools, PCB Design Tutorial Rev A, Copper for PCBs, Saturn PCB Toolkit and IPC-2152 (Section 05))*
3. **Laminate datasheets → design numbers** — For three laminates your fab offers (a standard FR-4, a high-Tg/high-Td FR-4, and a low-loss or hydrocarbon-ceramic RF material; Isola 370HR and Rogers RO4350B are worked examples of the last two), extract Dk and Df at the highest listed frequency, Tg, Td, z-axis CTE below and above Tg, and moisture absorption. Compute dielectric loss at 1 and 5 GHz (α ≈ 2.3 · f(GHz) · Df · √Dk dB/in) and the z-axis growth of a 1.6 mm board from 25 °C to 260 °C versus a copper via barrel (~17 ppm/°C). Deliverable: a one-page memo choosing a material for (i) a 6-layer MCU/USB/Ethernet board, (ii) a 2.4 GHz antenna feed, and (iii) a low-leakage precision analog board, each justified by the deciding numbers. *(~3 h · Isola 370HR and Rogers RO4350B data sheets, PCB Material Selection, IPC-4101 slash sheets, Altium Academy's low-Dk video; IPC-2221A Tables 4-1 and 6-2 list typical dielectric properties if you have the paid standard)*
4. **Red-pen the 2004 tutorial** — Read *PCB Design Tutorial Rev A* end to end and tabulate ≥ 12 of its claims as *still right*, *outdated process*, or *wrong/contested*, each with one line of reasoning and a source. Include at least: "always use imperial units", "generating Gerbers is a thing of the past", split planes for star grounding, "six layers = four signal + two power", "right-angle corners cause no measurable EMI", "one bypass capacitor per power pin", "route pin → capacitor → plane", HAL as the standard finish, the critical-length rule, and the crosstalk formula 1/(1+(D/H)²). Done when every *wrong/contested* verdict cites something stronger than opinion — e.g., IPC-2221A §6.4.4 ("reference planes … should not be segmented"; paid standard), Rick Hartley's grounding lecture or ADI's MT-031 (both in Section 05), or a measurement. *(~3 h · PCB Design Tutorial Rev A, Hartley's How to Achieve Proper Grounding and MT-031 (Section 05), IPC-2221 (paid, optional))*

**🏗️ Mini-project — Fab Notes for a Project Template:** Write the fabrication-drawing notes block for a KiCad project template of your own (see Section 02): base material (Tg/Td class and IPC-4101 slash sheet), finished thickness and tolerance, outer/inner copper weights, minimum hole-wall plating, surface finish (ENIG vs lead-free HASL vs ImAg/OSP, with the deciding reason), mask and silk, performance class (Class 1, 2 or 3, as defined in IPC-2221A §1.6.2; paid standard), impedance-control note format, bow and twist, electrical test, and marking. Give every note a one-line rationale. Send a quote request with these notes to two fabs (one low-cost offshore, one domestic quick-turn). Deliverable: `projects/pcb/01-fab-notes/` with the notes, both quotes, and every engineering question (EQ) you received plus its resolution. Success: both fabs quote with no open EQs, and the block drops straight into the fab drawing of your project template. *(~6 h)*

## ✅ Check Yourself

Answer from memory first, then check against the sources — or paste this section into NotebookLM and ask it to quiz you.

1. What are cores and prepregs, and which one's final thickness depends on the copper pattern pressed into it?
2. State 1 oz copper's thickness and approximate sheet resistance, then estimate the resistance of a 0.25 mm × 40 mm trace in 1 oz copper.
3. Per IPC-2221A Appendix B, where did the external-conductor current chart come from, and how were the internal-layer curves derived?
4. Why does z-axis CTE above Tg matter more to via reliability than Tg itself?
5. Estimate the dielectric loss of 10 in of FR-4 (Dk 4.2, Df 0.02) at 1 GHz. Does it matter for USB 2.0 High-Speed? For a 10 Gb/s serial link?
6. Misconception probe: "FR-4 is one material with Dk = 4.5." What actually varies, and by how much? (Compare the 370HR data sheet's Dk at 100 MHz and at 10 GHz; IPC-2221A Table 6-2 lists ranges by resin and glass if you have the paid standard.)
7. Name three surface finishes and a situation that favors each.
8. What does an IPC-4101 slash sheet such as /126 fix about a laminate, and why name the slash sheet on a fab drawing rather than a brand?
9. Explain to a smart friend in 3 sentences what is physically inside a 6-layer PCB and how its layers get connected.
