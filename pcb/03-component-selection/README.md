# Component Selection and Sourcing

> **Why this section matters:** Choosing the right parts determines whether your board can actually be built.

**Prerequisites:** [Section 00 — Electronics Fundamentals](../00-electronics-fundamentals/README.md)

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

After this section you can:
- Select a precision part from parametric search by computing worst-case error and noise against the requirement — designing to max-over-temperature specs, not typicals.
- Predict effective component values in circuit: MLCC capacitance under DC bias, temperature, and aging; resistor TCR and power/voltage derating; op-amp bias current vs temperature.
- Score supply-chain risk (lifecycle, sourcing breadth, stock, lead time, verified form-fit-function alternates) and decide which BOM lines may be substituted and which must be locked.
- Read package names and outline drawings (pitch, body, height, exposed pad, JEDEC outline reference) and translate them into fab, assembly, and inspection consequences.

## Suggested Order

**Core path (🔑):** DigiKey / Mouser parametric search → Design for Supply Chain

1. **DigiKey / Mouser parametric search** — Learn to filter by specs, stock, and lifecycle status; this is a daily skill.
2. **Maxim AN5527 (MLCC derating)** — Read it before trusting any MLCC's nominal value, then do Practice 2.
3. **Phil's Lab component selection guides** — Watch practical walkthroughs of choosing regulators, MCUs, passives, etc.
4. **Octopart** — Use for cross-distributor price and availability comparisons.
5. **JLCPCB Parts Library** — Check part availability early if targeting JLCPCB assembly.
6. **JEDEC standards overview** — Familiarize yourself with package naming so you can read datasheets fluently.
7. **Design for Supply Chain** — Internalize the practice of choosing multi-sourced, in-production parts from the start.
8. **J-STD-048A / J-STD-046A** — Learn what EOL and change notices must contain, then write your lifecycle triggers into the mini-project's sourcing policy.

## 🛠️ Practice

1. **Package literacy drill** — For ten packages on or near your boards (LQFP-100 0.5 mm, UFQFPN-48, a 0.4 mm-pitch WLCSP, SOT-23-6 vs SC-70-6 vs SOT-563, an exposed-pad VSON, TSSOP-20, 0201 vs 0402, a 0.5 mm-pitch BGA), record body size, pitch, height, the JEDEC outline the package drawing cites, whether it forces via-in-pad or tighter-than-standard trace/space, how its joints get inspected (AOI vs X-ray), and whether the JLCPCB Parts Library stocks it for assembly. Deliverable: a table with a "does this raise my fab or assembly class?" column. *(~2 h · JEDEC standards overview, JLCPCB Parts Library)*
2. **MLCC derating, quantified** — Find the smallest package that delivers ≥ 4.7 µF *effective* at 3.3 V bias and 85 °C on an LDO output, comparing 0402/0603/0805 at 6.3/10/16/25 V in X5R and X7R. Distributor filters show nominal values only, so read effective capacitance from the manufacturers' characteristic data (Murata, TDK, Samsung, KEMET tools). Deliverable: nominal-vs-effective table + pick, checked against the LDO's stability window (minimum C, ESR range). Then write three sentences on why the same part is a poor coupling or timing capacitor in a high-gain, sub-10 Hz sensor amplifier, and what you would use instead. *(~2 h · Maxim AN5527, DigiKey/Mouser parametric search, Phil's Lab component selection guides)*
3. **Op-amp shoot-out for a high-Z buffer** — Requirement: buffer a 1 GΩ source with < 1 % of a 1 nA full scale (≈ 10 pA) total input-current error from 0–70 °C, on a single 3.3 V supply at < 2 mA. Shortlist four candidates with DigiKey/Mouser parametric filters and tabulate I_B (typ/max at 25 °C, max at 70 °C — or extrapolated at ~2× per 10 °C for CMOS/JFET inputs), V_OS and drift, e_n at 1 Hz and 1 kHz, input common-mode range, price, stock, lifecycle, and second sources. Deliverable: table + pick + the error budget that justifies it. Done when your pick meets the requirement on max specs and you have flagged one "typical-only" trap in a rival's datasheet. *(~3 h · DigiKey/Mouser parametric search, Phil's Lab component selection guides)*
4. **Automated BOM risk scoring** — Export a BOM from one of your boards and write a Python script against the Nexar (Octopart) or DigiKey/Mouser APIs that pulls lifecycle, authorized stock, lead time, and price breaks, then scores each line (single source, < 1 k in stock, NRND/EOL, lead time > 12 weeks, no form-fit-function alternate). Deliverable: script + scored CSV + the top five risks, each with a proposed alternate. Done when every alternate is verified pin-for-pin and spec-for-spec against its datasheet, not just matched parametrically. *(~4 h · Octopart, DigiKey/Mouser parametric search, Design for Supply Chain, J-STD-048A/046A)*

**🏗️ Mini-project — Release BOM with Locked and Flexible Lines:** For one STM32U5-class board, build a release BOM in which every line carries MPN, one or two *verified* alternates (pinout, footprint, and electrical), lifecycle, a stock snapshot, roll-up cost at 10/100/1k, JLCPCB basic/extended status, and a `substitution` field: `free` (generic passives), `approved-alternates-only`, or `locked` (sense resistors, references, low-leakage and C0G signal-path caps, magnetics). Store alternates and the substitution policy as symbol fields in your library so they flow into `kicad-cli` BOM exports. Deliverable: `projects/pcb/03-release-bom/` with the BOM, its generator script, and a one-page sourcing policy (including what an EOL notice or PCN under J-STD-048A/046A triggers). Success: in a "line-down drill" where you knock out three random lines, the board stays buildable without a re-spin. *(~8 h)*

## ✅ Check Yourself

Answer from memory first, then check against the sources — or paste this section into NotebookLM and ask it to quiz you.

1. Typical vs max vs "guaranteed by design": which do you design to, and what (if anything) does "typical" tell you statistically?
2. A CMOS op-amp's input bias current roughly doubles every ____ °C. Estimate the factor between 25 °C and 85 °C.
3. Define Active, NRND, EOL, and Last Time Buy, and what each should trigger in your design process.
4. Why might two "pin-compatible" LDOs fail as drop-in replacements? Give three reasons.
5. Estimate the remaining capacitance of a 10 µF, 6.3 V, 0603 X5R at 5 V DC bias. Where do you get the real number?
6. Misconception probe: "X7R is a stable dielectric, so it's fine in a high-gain, low-frequency signal path." Which three effects say otherwise?
7. What does a design-for-supply-chain policy look like for generic passives versus single-source ICs?
8. Explain to a smart friend in 3 sentences why the cheapest BOM rarely produces the cheapest product.
