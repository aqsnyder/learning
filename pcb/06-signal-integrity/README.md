# Signal Integrity

## Why This Section Matters

As frequencies rise, traces become transmission lines and physics takes over. Signal integrity is the discipline that bridges PCB layout with electromagnetic theory. Understanding impedance, reflections, crosstalk, and return paths is what separates a board that works on the bench from one that works reliably in production. This is where the books on Rick Hartley's reading list become essential.

## Prerequisites

- [Section 04 — PCB Layout Fundamentals](../04-pcb-layout-fundamentals/README.md)
- [Section 05 — Power Distribution and Grounding](../05-power-and-grounding/README.md)
- Optional theory: [Physics Section 03 — Electromagnetism](../../physics/03-electromagnetism/README.md) (Maxwell's equations and field energy, the foundations behind this section and Section 07)

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

After this section you can:
- Decide from rise time and length whether an interconnect is electrically long, and predict reflections and ringing from source/load mismatch with a lattice (bounce) diagram.
- Calculate single-ended and differential impedance with closed-form approximations and a 2D field solver, and explain each sensitivity — dielectric height, width, copper thickness, Dk, solder mask.
- Choose and size terminations (series, parallel, AC, differential) for MCU GPIO/SPI, USB 2.0, and 100BASE-TX, and show with numbers when none is needed.
- Quantify near- and far-end crosstalk against spacing, height, coupled length, and microstrip vs stripline, and turn the result into spacing rules.
- Specify controlled-impedance nets and differential pairs to a fab and in KiCad: width/gap, tolerance, reference layer, and skew or time-domain constraints.

## Suggested Order

**Core path (🔑):** Bogatin's *Signal and Power Integrity — Simplified* → Bogatin's videos on Feranec's channel (reflections, crosstalk, differential pairs) → Johnson & Graham's *Black Magic*

1. **Signal and Power Integrity — Simplified** — Start with Bogatin; read at least the first half for core concepts
2. **Bogatin's videos on Feranec's channel** — Reflections, crosstalk and differential pairs, each next to the matching chapter
3. **Return paths and reference planes** — Re-watch Hartley's grounding lecture (Section 05) with transmission lines in mind: the return current in the plane under the trace is half the circuit
4. **Differential pairs and controlled impedance** — With Bogatin's differential-pair video and TI SPRAAR7: when differential signaling is required, how pairs are routed and referenced, and how to specify impedance to your fab (layer, reference, target, tolerance, coupon; stackups are Section 08's topic)
5. **TI SPRAAR7 + SNLA387** — Apply those rules to the interfaces you actually ship (USB 2.0/3.x, Ethernet MDI and magnetics)
6. **High-Speed Digital Design: Black Magic** — Deep dive into the classic handbook; use as reference alongside practical work
7. **High-Speed Digital System Design (Hall, Hall & McCall)** — A second, more compact treatment of interconnect theory and practice
8. **Right the First Time** — Physics-first design rules from Lee Ritchey
9. **High-Speed Signal Propagation: Advanced Black Magic** — Lossy-line behavior once your edges and channels demand it
10. **UltraCAD Calculators** — Verify your impedance and trace geometry calculations

## 🛠️ Practice

1. **Bounce diagram vs SPICE** — Model an STM32 GPIO driving 15 cm of 50 Ω microstrip into a 5 pF CMOS load. Get the driver's effective output resistance and edge rate from ST's IBIS model (the `[Pullup]`/`[Pulldown]` V–I tables and `[Ramp]`). Hand-draw the lattice diagram for that source resistance and again with a series resistor chosen to match the line, then simulate both in LTspice with a lossless transmission line. Deliverable: hand vs SPICE overlay. Done when your hand-predicted overshoot and settling agree with SPICE within ~10 % and you can state the maximum unterminated length for this edge rate from Bogatin's rule of thumb. *(~3 h · Bogatin's Signal and Power Integrity — Simplified (reflections) and his reflections video, Johnson & Graham's High-Speed Digital Design (terminations), LTspice (Section 00))*
2. **Write a 2D field solver** — In ~100 lines of NumPy/SciPy, solve Laplace's equation on a nonuniform grid for a microstrip, compute capacitance with and without the dielectric, and get Z₀ = 1/(c·√(C·C_air)) and ε_eff = C/C_air. Use a fab's published 4-layer stackup (e.g., ~0.2 mm 7628 prepreg). Compare against the UltraCAD calculators, Saturn PCB Toolkit, KiCad's TransLine calculator, and the fab's own impedance calculator (and IPC-2221A's closed form, §6.4.1, if you have the paid standard); then add a 15–25 µm solder-mask layer. Deliverable: notebook + comparison table. Done when grid refinement moves Z₀ by < 0.5 %, your solver agrees with Saturn within ~3 %, and you can explain why closed-form and mask-free calculators disagree with you by 1–3 Ω. *(~5 h · Bogatin's Signal and Power Integrity — Simplified (impedance), UltraCAD Calculators, KiCad Calculator Tools (Section 01), Saturn PCB Toolkit (Section 08))*
3. **Crosstalk vs spacing** — Extend your solver to two traces (extract the C and L matrices) or use Saturn's crosstalk calculator, and compute the saturated backward-crosstalk coefficient K_b = ¼(C_m/C + L_m/L) for two 50 Ω microstrips at s = h, 2h, 3h, and for the same geometry in stripline. Compare with Johnson & Graham's K/(1+(D/H)²) estimate (mind whether each formula uses edge or center spacing), then simulate one case in SPICE with coupled lines to see NEXT and FEXT waveforms. Deliverable: NEXT-vs-s/h plot (formula, solver, SPICE) and a KiCad spacing rule for clocks near sensitive analog nets. Done when SPICE NEXT matches K_b within ~10 % and you can explain why stripline FEXT is near zero. *(~4 h · Bogatin's Signal and Power Integrity — Simplified (crosstalk) and his crosstalk video, Johnson & Graham's High-Speed Digital Design, Ritchey's Right the First Time)*
4. **Spec the pairs** — First decide, with rise-time-vs-length numbers, whether your USB link (Full-Speed vs High-Speed) and your SPI lines to an Ethernet controller need impedance control at all. Then, for USB 2.0 (90 Ω) and 100BASE-TX MDI (100 Ω) on a 6-layer stackup, compute width/gap on outer and inner layers (Saturn plus your solver), choose loose vs tight coupling, and derive intra-pair skew limits from each interface's rise time. Encode the result as KiCad netclass/diff-pair rules (time-domain constraints if you are on KiCad 10) and a fab-drawing impedance table (layer, reference, target, tolerance, coupon). Deliverable: rules + fab note. *(~3 h · Bogatin's differential-pair video, TI SPRAAR7 and SNLA387, KiCad 10 PCB Editor manual (differential pairs, length tuning; Section 04))*

**🏗️ Mini-project — SI Test-Coupon Board:** Design a small 4-layer board with SMA edge launches carrying: 50 Ω microstrip and stripline in two lengths (≥ 15 cm, so a 1.5–3 GHz VNA can resolve them), 90 Ω and 100 Ω pairs, a line crossing a reference-plane gap, a layer change with and without adjacent stitching vias, a ~30 mm open stub (its quarter-wave notch near 1.3 GHz falls inside a NanoVNA's range; a 10 mm stub's would sit near 4 GHz, out of range), and coupled lines at s = h and s = 3h. Predict every structure (your solver + SPICE), then measure with a NanoVNA-class VNA (S11/S21, converted to TDR with scikit-rf's time-domain tools) and your scope. Deliverable: KiCad project, prediction notebook, and a measurement report with predicted-vs-measured overlays in `projects/pcb/06-si-coupons/`. Success: TDR-derived Z₀ within ±10 % of prediction for every line, and the plane-gap and missing-stitch structures measurably worse than their controls. *(~15 h)*
