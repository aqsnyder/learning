### Reasoning and Test-Time Compute

| Priority | Type | Resource | Author(s) | Year | Link | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🔑 | 📄 Paper (free) | Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters | Snell et al. | 2024 | [arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314) | Compares two uses of inference compute, search against process-reward verifiers and sequential revision, and shows when they beat a bigger model. | 🔑 | 📄 Paper (free) | DeepSeek-R1: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability in LLMs via Reinforcement Learning | DeepSeek-AI et al. | 2025 | [arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948) | R1-Zero gets long chain-of-thought reasoning from RL alone (GRPO, rule-based rewards); R1 adds cold-start SFT and more stages. Introduces the paradigm; few technical details. As of October 2026 the page marks o1 itself as deprecated: read it for the idea, not the model. |

Reasoning models are trained with large-scale reinforcement learning to think in long chains of thought, and their accuracy keeps improving as they are allowed more inference-time compute. OpenAI's o1 announcement introduced the paradigm. Snell et al. formalizes when test-time compute scaling beats simply training a larger model. DeepSeek-R1 openly demonstrated reinforcement-learning-driven chain-of-thought at scale (for the RL recipes behind it — GRPO and RL with verifiable rewards, plus the process-reward alternative it did not adopt — see [Section 10](../10-reinforcement-learning/README.md)).

Multimodal models unify vision, language, and other modalities into a single architecture. CLIP pioneered contrastive image-text pretraining, SAM brought foundation-model thinking to segmentation, LLaVA showed the simple open recipe (vision encoder + projection + LLM, trained on visual instruction data) that many later open vision-language models built on, and Gemini represented the natively multimodal frontier at its 2023 release.

### Self-Supervised Learning

Self-supervised learning extracts rich representations from unlabeled data, dramatically reducing the need for labeled datasets. Contrastive methods (SimCLR, MoCo) learn by comparing augmented views, self-distillation (DINO) learns without negative pairs, and masked prediction (MAE, JEPA) reconstructs missing information. These objectives let a vision backbone learn from unlabeled images, so downstream tasks need far fewer labels.

| Priority | Type | Resource | Author(s) | Year | Link | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | 📄 Paper (free) | SimCLR — A Simple Framework for Contrastive Learning of Visual Representations | Chen et al. | 2020 | [arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709) | Contrastive learning between two augmented views of each image. Shows that the augmentation mix, a nonlinear projection head and large batches all matter. |
| | 📄 Paper (free) | MoCo v2 — Improved Baselines with Momentum Contrastive Learning | Chen et al. | 2020 | [arxiv.org/abs/2003.04297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.04297) | Short note adding SimCLR's MLP projection head and stronger augmentation to MoCo's momentum encoder and negative queue; it outperforms SimCLR without large batches. |
| | 📄 Paper (free) | DINO — Emerging Properties in Self-Supervised Vision Transformers; DINOv2: Learning Robust Visual Features without Supervision | Caron et al.; Oquab et al. | 2021, 2023 | [DINO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14294) · [DINOv2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07193) | DINO: a student network matches a momentum teacher without negatives; its ViT attention maps outline objects. DINOv2 scales data and model for all-purpose frozen features. |
| | 📄 Paper (free) | MAE — Masked Autoencoders Are Scalable Vision Learners | He et al. | 2021 | [arxiv.org/abs/2111.06377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.06377) | Masks a high fraction (e.g. 75%) of image patches and reconstructs the pixels; the encoder sees only visible patches, so pretraining is fast. |
| | 📄 Paper (free) | I-JEPA — Self-Supervised Learning from Images with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture | Assran et al. | 2023 | [arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243) | Predicts representations of masked target blocks from one context block, not pixels, and needs no hand-crafted augmentations. Compare with MAE's pixel reconstruction. |

### Alternative Architectures

The transformer's quadratic attention cost has spurred a wave of subquadratic alternatives. State space models (S4, Mamba) achieve linear-time sequence modeling, while RWKV and xLSTM revisit recurrence with modern training tricks. These architectures are particularly compelling for long-context and real-time applications.

| Priority | Type | Resource | Author(s) | Year | Link | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | 📄 Paper (free) | S4 — Efficiently Modeling Long Sequences with Structured State Spaces | Gu, Goel & Ré | 2021 | [arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00396) | Makes long-range state-space models practical with a structured (normal plus low-rank) parameterization; first to solve LRA's Path-X. Source for Practice 1's recurrence/convolution duality. Also used in math/07. |
| 🔑 | 📄 Paper (free) | Mamba: Linear-Time Sequence Modeling with Selective State Spaces | Gu & Dao | 2023 | [arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00752) | Makes SSM parameters input-dependent ('selection'), giving up the convolution view for a hardware-aware parallel scan. Linear-time and competitive with transformers on language. |
| | 📄 Paper (free) | RWKV: Reinventing RNNs for the Transformer Era | Peng et al. | 2023 | [arxiv.org/abs/2305.13048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13048) | A linear-attention RNN that trains in parallel like a transformer but runs with constant memory and compute per token at inference; scaled to 14B parameters. |
| | 📄 Paper (free) | xLSTM: Extended Long Short-Term Memory | Beck et al. | 2024 | [arxiv.org/abs/2405.04517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04517) | Revives the LSTM with exponential gating and new memory cells (scalar sLSTM, parallelizable matrix mLSTM), stacked into residual blocks. Tests whether scaled recurrence rivals transformers. |

### Agentic AI

LLM-powered agents combine reasoning, tool use, memory, and retrieval to act autonomously. ReAct interleaves reasoning traces with actions, RAG grounds generation in retrieved documents (Sentence-BERT explains the sentence embeddings that semantic retrieval is built on), Tree of Thoughts explores branching reasoning paths, and Toolformer teaches models to call APIs on their own. "Building Effective Agents" distills the practical design patterns (prompt chaining, routing, orchestrator–workers, evaluator–optimizer, autonomous loops), and SWE-bench made resolving real GitHub issues a widely used test of coding agents. (Sparks of AGI is a capabilities-evaluation study of GPT-4 rather than an agents paper; it sits here for historical reasons.)

| Priority | Type | Resource | Author(s) | Year | Link | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | 📄 Paper (free) | ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models | Yao et al. | 2022 | [arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629) | Interleaves reasoning traces with actions such as Wikipedia lookups, reducing chain-of-thought hallucination and error propagation. The Thought-Action-Observation loop of Practice 4. |
| | 📄 Paper (free) | RAG — Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks | Lewis et al. | 2020 | [arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.11401) | Pairs a pretrained seq2seq generator with a neural retriever over a dense Wikipedia index, fine-tuned jointly. Grounds generation in retrieved text, not parameters alone. |
| | 📄 Paper (free) | Tree of Thoughts: Deliberate Problem Solving with Large Language Models | Yao et al. | 2023 | [arxiv.org/abs/2305.10601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10601) | Generalizes chain-of-thought into tree search: the model proposes and self-evaluates intermediate thoughts, with lookahead and backtracking. Prompt-level search, before RL-trained reasoners. |

### Robotics and Embodied AI

Foundation models are increasingly being applied to physical-world reasoning and control. RT-2 connects vision-language models directly to robot actions, π0 pairs a pretrained vision-language model with a flow-matching action head to control many different robots, and World Models learn compressed latent dynamics for planning and imagination.

| Priority | Type | Resource | Author(s) | Year | Link | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | 📄 Paper (free) | RT-2: Vision-Language-Action Models Transfer Web Knowledge to Robotic Control | Brohan et al. | 2023 | [arxiv.org/abs/2307.15818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15818) | Co-fine-tunes a vision-language model on web data and robot trajectories, emitting actions as text tokens. Names the class: vision-language-action (VLA) models. |
| | 📄 Paper (free) | World Models | Ha & Schmidhuber | 2018 | [arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122) | Learns a compressed latent model of an environment (VAE plus mixture-density RNN) and trains a small controller, even entirely inside the model's own 'dream'. |
| | 📄 Paper (free) | π0: A Vision-Language-Action Flow Model for General Robot Control | Black et al. | 2024 | [arxiv.org/abs/2410.24164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24164) | Adds a flow-matching action head to a pretrained vision-language model, trained on many robot platforms. A generalist policy for dexterous tasks like laundry folding. |

## 🛠️ Practice

1. **State-space models are filters you already know** — Take a continuous SSM x′ = Ax + Bu, y = Cx with N = 16 (the HiPPO-LegS A from the S4 paper, or any stable A). Discretize it with the bilinear transform (S4's choice) and with zero-order hold (Mamba's). Compute the output for a 4,096-sample input both as a recurrence and as a convolution with the kernel K̄ = (CB̄, CĀB̄, CĀ²B̄, …), and plot the kernel's impulse and frequency response — it is an IIR filter. Then make the step size Δ depend on the input (Mamba's selection) and show the convolution view no longer applies while the recurrence still does. Done when: recurrence and convolution agree to 1e-6 in the time-invariant case and you can explain why Mamba needs a hardware-aware parallel scan. *(~4 h · S4; Mamba)*
2. **Representations without labels** — On CIFAR-10, compare frozen features from (a) OpenCLIP ViT-B/32 — zero-shot with bare class names, with "a photo of a {label}.", and with a 5-prompt ensemble, plus a linear probe; (b) DINOv2 ViT-S/14 — linear probe and k-NN; (c) a ResNet-18 SimCLR you pretrain yourself for ~30 epochs, ablating color jitter and the projection head. Finally, run zero-shot CLIP on 50 phone photos of bench gear — oscilloscope, multimeter, soldering iron, bare PCB, breadboard. Done when: you have one results table, can explain each ranking with the papers' design choices, and have characterized where CLIP fails on your photos. *(~6 h incl. GPU time · CLIP; SimCLR; MoCo v2; DINO/DINOv2)*
3. **Test-time compute on a small model** — With a 0.5–1.5B open instruct model on 300 GSM8K problems, sample N ∈ {1, 2, 4, …, 64} solutions and plot accuracy vs. generated tokens for majority voting and for best-of-N under a verifier (an open reward/verifier model, or a self-evaluation prompt). Compare with a ~7B model at N = 1, matching FLOPs (≈ 2 × parameters × tokens). Bucket problems by difficulty using the small model's own pass rate, as Snell et al. do. Done when: you can say whether your data supports their claim that test-time compute can substitute for parameters on easier problems but not on the hardest ones. *(~6 h · Snell et al. 2024)*
4. **Agent from parts: RAG + ReAct over datasheets** — Build a ~200-line ReAct loop (Thought → Action → Observation) with two tools: retrieval over 20 datasheets and app notes you actually use (chunked, embedded with an open embedding model, cosine top-k — the RAG pattern) and a Python calculator. Write 30 questions with known answers that need lookup plus arithmetic (e.g. "worst-case offset error at a gain of 100 for part X over 0–70 °C"). Compare closed-book, RAG-only and RAG + ReAct. Done when: you've reported accuracy for all three and classified every failure as retrieval miss, reasoning error or tool misuse. *(~6 h · ReAct; RAG; Sentence-BERT; Toolformer)*
5. **Claims ledger** — Write a 300-word critique of *Sparks of AGI*'s methodology — qualitative probes of an early, unreleased model without a fixed protocol — ending with what a falsifiable version of its central claim would look like. Then, using the latest Stanford AI Index trend data, write five predictions about this section's threads that resolve within 12 months, each with a probability, a resolution criterion and a date, in a file you'll revisit. Done when: a stranger could resolve every prediction without asking you what you meant. *(~2 h · Sparks of AGI; Stanford HAI AI Index)*

**🏗️ Mini-project — R1-Zero at Toy Scale:** RL-train a 0.5–1.5B *base* model on a task with verifiable answers — Countdown (combine given numbers to hit a target) or GSM8K — using GRPO with only rule-based rewards (answer correctness plus format), as in DeepSeek-R1-Zero. Use TRL's `GRPOTrainer` or write GRPO yourself (group-normalized advantages, no critic). Track reward, held-out accuracy, mean response length, and how often self-checking phrases ("wait", "let me verify") appear over training. Deliverable: `projects/ml/11-frontiers/r1-zero-toy/` with plots, 10 annotated transcripts from early vs. late training, and a one-page comparison with the paper's training-dynamics figures. Done when: held-out accuracy improves by ≥ 10 points over the base model with the same prompt, and you can say — with examples — whether response length grew and whether anything like the paper's "aha moment" appeared. *(~15 h incl. GPU time on one 24–80 GB GPU · DeepSeek-R1; GRPO from DeepSeekMath in [Section 10](../10-reinforcement-learning/README.md))*
