# Historical Foundations of AI

## Why This Section Matters

Understanding the history of artificial intelligence helps you appreciate *why* certain ideas succeeded while others stalled for decades. Many "modern" breakthroughs — neural networks, backpropagation, even attention — have roots stretching back to the 1940s and 1950s. By studying the intellectual lineage, you'll see how constraints in compute, data, and theory shaped the field's trajectory, and you'll develop the intuition to recognize which ideas are genuinely novel versus rediscoveries with better hardware.

## Suggested Order

1. **Start with the blog** — Read Kurenkov's history series to get the full narrative arc before diving into primary sources.
2. **McCulloch & Pitts (1943)** — See where the idea of a mathematical neuron began.
3. **Turing (1950)** — Understand the philosophical framing that launched AI as a discipline.
4. **Rosenblatt (1958)** — Learn about the first trainable neural network.
5. **Minsky & Papert (1969)** — Understand why progress stalled (the "AI winter").
6. **Rumelhart, Hinton & Williams (1986)** — See how backpropagation broke the deadlock and made deep networks trainable.
