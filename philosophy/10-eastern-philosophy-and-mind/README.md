# Eastern Philosophy and Mind

## Why This Section Matters

Western philosophy of mind typically assumes a unified, persistent self — but Buddhist philosophy has questioned this assumption for 2,500 years. The Buddhist doctrine of anattā (non-self) holds that there is no fixed, unchanging self — just a constantly changing stream of physical and mental processes. This is not mere metaphysics: it has deep implications for consciousness studies, personal identity, and AI. If there is no essential self, what are we trying to replicate in AI? This section bridges Eastern and Western traditions, showing how Buddhist insights complement and challenge the Western assumptions underlying the rest of this curriculum.

2. **Siderits — Buddhism as Philosophy** — Bridge to Western analytic philosophy
3. **SEP — Buddha** — Philosophical context and significance

5. **Thompson — Waking, Dreaming, Being** — Consciousness through an East-West lens

7. **Flanagan — The Bodhisattva's Brain** — Naturalist critique and appreciation
8. **Wright — Buddhist and Modern Psychology (Coursera)** — Accessible video companion
