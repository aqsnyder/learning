# Free Will and Agency

## Why This Section Matters

If human minds are physical systems governed by natural laws, do we have free will? And if we don't, what happens to moral responsibility, praise, blame, and punishment? This question takes on new urgency in the age of AI: if we build autonomous agents, are they responsible for their actions? Can an AI be blameworthy? Dennett's compatibilism is the most influential reconciliation of free will with determinism, but the debate is far from settled — Libet's neuroscience experiments, Frankfurt's thought experiments, and Sapolsky's biological determinism all challenge our intuitions about control and responsibility.

## 🛠️ Practice

1. **The Consequence Argument** — Reconstruct it in standard form (no one has power over the remote past or the laws of nature; if determinism is true, our acts are consequences of the past plus the laws; so no one has power over their acts), making the transfer-of-powerlessness principle explicit (van Inwagen's "Rule β"). Using the SEP *Free Will* and *Compatibilism* entries, evaluate the two classic compatibilist targets: the analysis of "could have done otherwise", and the transfer principle itself. 500 words, ending with the premise you reject, if any, and what rejecting it costs you. Check: the argument is valid with β stated; without β it isn't. *(~3 h · SEP Free Will; SEP Compatibilism)*
2. **Frankfurt cases, human and machine** — Restate the Principle of Alternate Possibilities and Frankfurt's case (Black stands ready to intervene; Jones decides on his own; Black never acts). Then build an AI analog: an LLM agent decides on its own to run a destructive command, while a monitoring classifier — the Black figure — would have forced the same command down any other path. Evaluate the "flicker of freedom" response and the dilemma about Black's prior sign (both covered in the SEP entries) for the human case and the AI case. Does the analogy hold, and is anyone responsible in the AI case? 600 words. *(~3 h · Frankfurt, "Alternate Possibilities and Moral Responsibility"; SEP Compatibilism)*
3. **Libet, simulated** — Summarize Libet's paradigm (the rotating-spot clock, the W and M judgments, the readiness potential) and its headline timings (RP onset roughly 550 ms before movement in unplanned acts; W roughly 200 ms before). Then implement Schurger, Sitt & Dehaene's leaky stochastic accumulator, dx = (I − kx)dt + c·dW, with a movement triggered when x crosses a threshold. Tune the parameters until waiting times are a few seconds, then average x time-locked backwards from threshold crossings: an RP-like ramp should appear from noise alone. 400 words: what does this show about inferring that "the brain decided first", and what Libet-style experiment could discriminate the accumulator account from the early-decision account? Compare Dennett's discussion of Libet in *Freedom Evolves*. *(~4 h · Libet 1983; Schurger, Sitt & Dehaene 2012; Dennett, Freedom Evolves)*
4. **Determinism without inevitability** — Implement Conway's Life in about 30 lines of NumPy and reproduce the set-up Dennett uses in *Freedom Evolves*: a glider meeting an eater. Then build a deterministic "agent" in a simple gridworld that uses a short lookahead to avoid a hazard, run it from 1,000 slightly perturbed initial conditions, and measure how robustly it avoids the hazard. 300 words: in what sense is the hazard avoidable though every run is fully determined, and is that the kind of freedom worth wanting in *Elbow Room*'s sense — or does Sapolsky's reply bite? *(~4 h · Dennett, Freedom Evolves; Dennett, Elbow Room; Sapolsky, Determined)*
5. **Sapolsky vs. Mitchell, with Dennett** — Write a 400-word steelman of Sapolsky's case against free will and desert (his chain of prior causes from neurons back through hormones, genes, upbringing and culture), and a 400-word steelman of Kevin Mitchell's case that organisms are genuine agents. Then 300 words placing Dennett: what does compatibilism concede to Sapolsky, and where does it part ways with Mitchell? Use Dennett's exchanges with Caruso in *Just Deserts* for his own replies to a free-will skeptic. Identify the exact claim on which each pair disagrees — the metaphysics of causation, or what "responsibility" is for. *(~3 h · Sapolsky, Determined; Mitchell, Free Agents; Dennett, Elbow Room; Dennett & Caruso, Just Deserts; Sapolsky, Human Behavioral Biology lectures)*

Use at least three criteria — alternate possibilities (PAP and Frankfurt), reasons-responsiveness (SEP *Compatibilism*), and Strawson's reactive attitudes ("Freedom and Resentment") — and take Matthias's responsibility gap from Section 06 as the challenge to answer.
