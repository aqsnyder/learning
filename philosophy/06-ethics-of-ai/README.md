# Ethics of AI

## Why This Section Matters

AI systems are already making decisions that affect hiring, criminal sentencing, loan approvals, and content moderation — often with biases baked into their training data and reward functions. The ethics of AI is not a future concern; it is an urgent present reality. This section covers fairness, accountability, transparency, dual use, and the moral status of AI systems. Liao's anthology provides the systematic framework, while O'Neil's *Weapons of Math Destruction* shows how algorithmic bias causes real harm to real people.

## Suggested Order

1. **Sandel — Justice (selected lectures)** — Build your moral reasoning toolkit
2. **O'Neil — Weapons of Math Destruction** — See how algorithmic bias causes real harm
3. **Liao (ed.) — Ethics of AI** — Systematic philosophical framework
4. **Jobin et al. — Global AI ethics guidelines** — Map the institutional landscape
5. **Barocas et al. — Fairness and ML** — Technical and philosophical foundations of fairness
6. **Crawford — Atlas of AI** — Material and political dimensions
7. **Eubanks — Automating Inequality** — Concrete case studies
8. **Benjamin — Race After Technology** — Structural analysis of tech and race
