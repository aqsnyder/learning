# Transactions and Script

## Why This Section Matters

Bitcoin transactions are programmable. Understanding the UTXO model, Script language, and transaction types is what separates surface understanding from deep knowledge.

## Prerequisites

- [Section 01 — Cryptography Foundations](../01-cryptography-foundations/README.md), [Section 02 — Protocol Fundamentals](../02-protocol-fundamentals/README.md), [Section 03 — Mining and Consensus](../03-mining-and-consensus/README.md)

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

After this section you can:
- Serialize and parse legacy and SegWit transactions byte by byte, computing txid, wtxid, weight and vsize.
- Implement a stack-based Script interpreter covering P2PKH, P2SH multisig, P2WPKH and P2WSH, and hand-trace its execution.
- Build, sign (legacy sighash and BIP 143) and broadcast transactions from your own code, using keys you generated yourself.
- Design and test timelocked spending paths with nLockTime, CLTV and CSV, and explain every rejection reason you hit.
- Explain how SegWit removed third-party malleability and how the witness discount changes fee arithmetic.

## Suggested Order

**Core path (🔑):** *Mastering Bitcoin* Chs. 6–7 → Learn Me A Bitcoin: Transactions → *Programming Bitcoin* Chs. 5–8 and 13

1. **Mastering Bitcoin Ch. 6–7** — Start with Antonopoulos and Harding for the conceptual framework of transactions and Script
2. **Learn Me A Bitcoin: Transactions** — Visualize and dissect real transactions; decode raw transactions by hand and check yourself with its transaction splitter or `bitcoin-cli decoderawtransaction`
3. **UTXO model** — Review inputs, outputs, change and coin selection with Learn Me A Bitcoin: UTXO and Mastering Bitcoin Ch. 2 (both listed in Section 02)
4. **Learn Me A Bitcoin: Bitcoin Script** — The stack-based execution model, opcodes, P2PKH, P2SH; step through scripts with **btcdeb**
5. **Programming Bitcoin Chs. 5–8 and 13** — Build your own transaction parser, Script interpreter and signer, then add SegWit
6. **BIP 141, BIP 143 and BIP 147** — Witness data, weight and the discount, the malleability fix, the new signature hash and NULLDUMMY
7. **BIPs 65, 68, 112 and 113** — CLTV, relative lock-time, CSV and median time-past
8. **bitcoin-tx-tutorial** — Hand-build and sign legacy, SegWit and Taproot transactions in Python against regtest (Taproot background: Mastering Bitcoin Ch. 7 and Section 11)
9. **Bitcoin Developer Reference** — Use as ongoing reference for raw transaction fields (frozen around 2021)

## 🛠️ Practice

1. **Two transactions, every byte** — On regtest, create one P2PKH spend (`getnewaddress "" legacy`) and one P2WPKH spend; dump the raw hex and annotate every field: version, marker and flag, varints, outpoints (little-endian txid + vout), scriptSig or witness items (DER signature + sighash byte, public key), sequence, amounts, scriptPubKeys, locktime. Compute txid, wtxid, weight and vsize yourself. Done when all four match `decoderawtransaction`. *(~2 h · Mastering Bitcoin ch. 6; Learn Me A Bitcoin: Transactions; Bitcoin Developer Reference: Transactions)*
2. **Fee arithmetic** — From field sizes alone, derive the vsize of a 1-input/2-output P2PKH, P2WPKH and P2TR key-path transaction (you should land near 226, 141 and 154 vB), then confirm against transactions you create on regtest. Compute each one's fee at 2 and 50 sat/vB and the percentage saved by SegWit and by Taproot, and explain where the savings come from. *(~1 h · Mastering Bitcoin chs. 6 and 9 (ch. 9 is listed in Section 06); BIP 141)*
3. **Script interpreter** — Implement *Programming Bitcoin*'s Script parser and evaluator (chs. 6 and 8): stack operations, OP_HASH160, OP_EQUAL(VERIFY), OP_CHECKSIG, OP_CHECKMULTISIG and P2SH redeem-script evaluation; pass the book's tests. Then hand-trace *Mastering Bitcoin*'s 2-of-3 multisig on paper and in your interpreter, including the extra element OP_CHECKMULTISIG pops and BIP 147's NULLDUMMY rule. *(~6 h · Programming Bitcoin chs. 6 and 8; Mastering Bitcoin ch. 7; Learn Me A Bitcoin: Bitcoin Script; BIP 147)*
4. **Your own signer** — On regtest (never with real funds), generate a key with your section-01 library and fund its P2PKH and P2WPKH addresses with `sendtoaddress` (Bitcoin Core ≥ 30 has no `dumpprivkey` — own your keys). Build and sign spends with your own code: legacy sighash for P2PKH, BIP 143 sighash for P2WPKH. Check your BIP 143 digest against the worked examples in BIP 143 before touching the node. Done when `testmempoolaccept` returns `allowed` and both transactions confirm. *(~5 h · Programming Bitcoin chs. 7 and 13; BIP 143)*
5. **Timelock lab** — Using your code or embit (Section 10), create P2WSH outputs locked by `<H+10> OP_CHECKLOCKTIMEVERIFY OP_DROP <pk> OP_CHECKSIG` and `<5> OP_CHECKSEQUENCEVERIFY OP_DROP <pk> OP_CHECKSIG`. Attempt each spend too early and with wrong nLockTime/nSequence values, recording every `testmempoolaccept` reject reason (`non-final`, `non-BIP68-final`, script-verification failures), then mine blocks and succeed. Deliverable: a table mapping each failure to the BIP 65/68/112/113 rule that caused it. *(~3 h · BIPs 65, 68, 112 and 113; Mastering Bitcoin ch. 7)*

**🏗️ Mini-project — Mohammed's Company Vault:** Implement *Mastering Bitcoin*'s "Complex Script Example" — 2-of-3 partners; the lawyer plus any one partner after 30 days; the lawyer alone after 90 days — as a P2WSH output on regtest, using block-based relative timelocks (30 and 90 blocks) so you can test quickly. Spend every path with your own signer, show that early spends fail with `non-BIP68-final`, and run every witness through your interpreter (watch P2WSH's MINIMALIF policy on the OP_IF selectors). Finally compute the time-based CSV encodings for real 30- and 90-day delays (type flag plus 512-second units). Success: all three paths confirmed; at least three invalid witnesses rejected by both your interpreter and Bitcoin Core for the same reason. *(~10 h · Mastering Bitcoin ch. 7 (Complex Script Example); BIPs 68 and 112)*

## ✅ Check Yourself

1. What does a SegWit txid commit to, and why does that eliminate third-party malleability?
2. Trace the stack for a P2PKH spend: what is on it after the scriptSig runs, after OP_DUP, and after OP_EQUALVERIFY?
3. A transaction has 200 bytes of non-witness data and 100 bytes of witness data. What are its weight and vsize?
4. Why does OP_CHECKMULTISIG need a dummy element, and what does BIP 147 require of it?
5. Contrast nLockTime, OP_CHECKLOCKTIMEVERIFY and OP_CHECKSEQUENCEVERIFY: absolute vs relative, transaction-level vs script-level.
6. "This transaction sends coins from address A to address B." Rewrite the sentence correctly in UTXO terms.
7. Where does the difference between total inputs and total outputs go, and what happens if you forget the change output?
8. Explain to a smart friend, in three sentences, how P2SH lets someone pay to a script they have never seen.
9. What does a BIP 143 signature hash commit to that the legacy one did not, and which two problems does that solve (one for validating nodes, one for offline signers)?
