# Mining and Consensus

> **Why this section matters:** Proof-of-work is what makes Bitcoin trustless. Understanding mining, difficulty adjustment, and Nakamoto consensus is essential.

## Prerequisites
- [01 - Cryptography Foundations](../01-cryptography-foundations/)
- [02 - Protocol Fundamentals](../02-protocol-fundamentals/)

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

After this section you can:
- Convert between `bits`, target and difficulty, and estimate network hashrate, power draw and solo-mining odds from live data.
- Implement the 2016-block retarget exactly (4× clamps, the 2015-interval off-by-one, the proof-of-work limit), explain how the timewarp attack abuses it, and state how BIP 94 and BIP 54 close it.
- Assemble and mine a valid block from `getblocktemplate`: coinbase with BIP 34 height and witness commitment, Merkle root, header, nonce search.
- Quantify double-spend risk against confirmations and attacker hashrate, and critique the Poisson approximation in the whitepaper.
- Analyze selfish mining and pool mechanics (Stratum jobs, extranonce, share difficulty), and explain why propagation and pool concentration matter.

## 🛠️ Practice

1. **Difficulty, hashrate, power** — From your node (`getblockheader`, `getdifficulty`) or mempool.space: expand the current `bits` into a target, compute difficulty, expected hashes per block (D × 2³²) and implied hashrate (÷ 600 s); compare with mempool.space's hashrate estimate. With a stated J/TH assumption, estimate network power (GW) and energy per block. Then compute the expected time for a 1 TH/s home miner to find a block solo, and the Nakamoto coefficient of today's pool distribution (fewest pools controlling > 50 %). *(~1.5 h · Mastering Bitcoin ch. 12; Mempool.space)*
2. **Retarget, exactly** — Implement `next_bits(prev_bits, t_first, t_last)` following *Programming Bitcoin*'s difficulty-adjustment section: clamp the timespan to 3.5 days–8 weeks, use the timestamps of the period's first and last blocks (the 2015-interval off-by-one), cap at the proof-of-work limit. Get mainnet headers from your node or by `getheaders` over P2P (2,000 per reply), and validate against every retarget boundary in the first 400,000 blocks. Done when every computed `bits` matches exactly. Then simulate a timewarp: show how a majority miner manipulating timestamps — while respecting median-time-past — drives difficulty down. Read BIP 94 (testnet4, Section 12) and BIP 54 (Consensus Cleanup, Section 11) and state the rule each adds to stop it. *(~3.5 h · Bitcoin Core src/pow.cpp; Programming Bitcoin chs. 9–10 (Section 02); Mastering Bitcoin ch. 12; Antonopoulos Q&As)*
3. **Mine a regtest block by hand** — Call `getblocktemplate '{"rules":["segwit"]}'` and build the coinbase yourself: BIP 34 height push (check how heights ≤ 16 encode and the 2–100-byte scriptSig rule), an extranonce, a payout to your own key, the `default_witness_commitment` output and the coinbase's 32-byte witness reserved value. Compute the Merkle root over the template's txids, assemble the header, grind the nonce until hash ≤ target, and `submitblock`. Done when `getbestblockhash` returns your block. Then break one thing at a time (Merkle root, bits, height push, timestamp) and record each reject reason. *(~4 h · Mastering Bitcoin ch. 12; Programming Bitcoin ch. 9 and Grokking Bitcoin ch. 7 (both Section 02))*
4. **Double-spend risk, revisited** — Extend your §11 port from section 00: (a) Monte Carlo the race, with honest and attacker blocks as competing Poisson processes, the attacker mining privately from the moment the merchant's payment is broadcast, and Satoshi's success criterion (the attacker's chain ever catches up — cap the deficit at ~100 to end hopeless runs); (b) implement the exact negative-binomial distribution of the attacker's head start; (c) tabulate the confirmations needed for P < 0.1 % at q = 0.10…0.45 under both. Done when the Monte Carlo agrees with the exact model within its error bars — you should find Satoshi's approximation understates the risk (6 rather than 5 confirmations at q = 0.10). *(~3 h · Bitcoin whitepaper §11 (Section 00); Mastering Bitcoin ch. 12 (Hashrate Attacks))*
5. **Selfish mining** — Simulate Eyal and Sirer's selfish-mining strategy as a state machine (private lead 0, 0′, 1, 2, …; γ = fraction of honest hashrate that mines on the selfish branch during a tie). Plot the selfish pool's revenue share against hashrate share α for γ ∈ {0, 0.5, 1}. Done when the break-even α matches (1 − γ)/(3 − 2γ) — 1/3, 1/4 and 0 — within simulation noise. Close with 150 words on what determines γ in practice and how network position and propagation speed shift it. *(~4 h · Eyal and Sirer, "Majority is not Enough"; Chaincode seminar session 3 (selfish-mining and propagation readings))*

**🏗️ Mini-project — Toy Stratum Pool:** Write a minimal Stratum v1 pool in Python on top of your regtest node's `getblocktemplate`/`submitblock` (`mining.subscribe`, `authorize`, `set_difficulty`, `notify`, `submit`), plus a CPU miner that rebuilds the coinbase from coinb1 + extranonce1 + extranonce2 + coinb2, folds in the Merkle branch, and grinds headers. The pool validates shares against the share target, rejects stale and duplicate shares, submits shares that meet the block target, and credits shares per worker. Optionally, read the Stratum component of ESP-Miner — the open-source ESP32 firmware for the Bitaxe home miner — to see how a real device turns `mining.notify` into ASIC jobs, including version rolling (BIP 310/320). Deliverable: `projects/btc/03-toy-pool/` plus a sequence diagram of one job. Success: blocks found through your pool are accepted (`submitblock` returns null) and pay the pool's address; bad shares are rejected with reasons; if you own a Bitaxe-class miner, it mines through your pool (rate-limit block submissions — regtest difficulty is trivial). *(~12 h · Mastering Bitcoin ch. 12 (Mining Pools); Chaincode seminar session 3 (Stratum and pool-reward readings))*
